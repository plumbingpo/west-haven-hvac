Close behind come cracked hot surface igniters, stuck pressure switches, clogged condensate traps on high-efficiency models, and blower motors that have quietly worn out. When to Call for Heating Repair in West Haven

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the fix. Watch for these telltale signs:

  • The furnace clicking and trying to light but shutting back down
  • Thick ice or frost covering the heat pump outside
  • Some rooms warm while others stay stubbornly cold
  • Frequent on-off cycles that never bring rooms up to temperature
  • Cold air, or no air at all, when the thermostat calls for heat
  • Booming or banging at ignition, a sign of delayed burner light-off
  • Strange smells from the vents, especially rotten egg or burning
  • Energy costs climbing even though the house feels colder

Why It Matters

Why Hire a Professional for Heating Repair?

Some of the most dangerous heating failures are the ones nobody notices. A cracked heat exchanger can send combustion gases into the supply air, a blocked or failing flue can spill carbon monoxide into the house, and a furnace that lights late booms gas inside the exchanger, weakening it a little more every cycle. An untrained eye rarely catches any of it. Even routine symptoms can mislead: a short-cycling furnace may have a bad limit switch, or it may simply be shutting down to protect itself from a choked filter. Clean a flame sensor without asking why it fouled, and the next lockout is already on the calendar.

That is why West Haven HVAC does not guess. Our technicians read flame signal on a meter, look at the heat exchanger directly, confirm the flue is drafting, and compare temperature rise to the data plate to make sure the blower speed fits the burner. On heat pumps we check defrost controls, sensors, and the reversing valve rather than blaming the compressor, and we find out whether the electric backup heat has been running unnoticed and driving up your costs. Nothing is called fixed until it has run a full cycle and proven itself safe and correct, because that is what the machine heating your home deserves.

Maintenance

Maintenance Tips for Heating Repair

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Install a CO detector on each floor and test them every month
  • Never store paints, solvents, or chlorine products near the furnace; their vapors corrode the burners
  • Give the outdoor heat pump unit open space, free of snow drifts and plantings, so it can defrost
  • Have the system serviced each autumn, before the weather tests every weak component
  • Take booming ignition or new rattles seriously; both are cheap to fix early and expensive late
  • Avoid crowding the furnace with storage, which can starve the burners of air
  • Change the filter monthly in heating season; starved airflow trips limit switches and cracks exchangers early
