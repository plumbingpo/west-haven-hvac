When the air conditioning quits in West Haven, CT, the house tells you fast, and so does everyone in it. The part that fails most often is the humble run capacitor: a few dollars of electronics that starts the compressor and fan, weakens quietly for years, and finally lets go on a brutal afternoon, leaving the outdoor unit humming but the fan still. Right behind it come pitted contactors that no longer pass power cleanly, condenser fan motors with dry bearings, refrigerant leaks that slowly bleed the system's capacity away, and evaporator coils that freeze into a block of ice whenever airflow collapses behind a clogged filter.

Warm air from the vents can mean a failed capacitor, a refrigerant leak, a frozen coil, or a duct problem, and each one carries a very different price to fix. That is why West Haven HVAC tests before touching anything. We meter capacitors against their microfarad rating, compare compressor and fan amp draws to the data plate, judge the refrigerant charge by superheat and subcooling instead of pressure alone, and inspect the coil, blower wheel, and filter whenever there is ice. Adding refrigerant without locating the leak only buys cold air until the charge leaks out again.

Honest advice is part of every visit. If an aging system's compressor has failed, we explain the repair-versus-replacement math plainly and let you decide. Most of the time, though, the fix is simpler, and because our trucks stock capacitors, contactors, fan motors, hard-start kits, and condensate pumps, cooling is usually restored before we leave. When to Call for AC Repair in West Haven

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • Water dripping from the indoor unit or showing up on the ceiling below
  • An iced-over evaporator coil or frozen refrigerant lines
  • Rapid on-off cycling that never brings the temperature down
  • Warm or room-temperature air blowing when the thermostat calls for cooling
  • The outdoor unit humming or clicking but the fan not spinning
  • A hot electrical smell or a tripped breaker whenever the air conditioner kicks on
  • A hiss or gurgle from the lines, a common sign of leaking refrigerant
  • Cooling bills climbing while the house stays sticky and warm

Why Professional AC Repair Matters

Consider the refrigerant first. It lives in a sealed, pressurized loop, venting it is prohibited under federal rules, and correct service requires gauges, a vacuum pump, and a recovery machine rather than a store-bought recharge kit. Add capacitors that hold a dangerous charge with the power off and a condensing unit fed by 240 volts, and the risks add up quickly. Then there are the diagnostic traps: an iced coil often points to poor airflow rather than low charge, and topping off a system with an unrepaired leak lets moisture and acid slowly damage the compressor while your money leaks out with the refrigerant.

Professional repair gets the whole picture. Our experienced technicians meter electrical components instead of swapping them, weigh charge decisions against measured superheat and subcooling, find leaks with detectors and dye rather than assumptions, and check the filter, blower, and duct static pressure whenever airflow is suspect. That is the difference between a repair that holds through the season and a parts-cannon fix that calls you back in three weeks. And when the system genuinely is not worth saving, we tell you that plainly too, with numbers instead of pressure.

Maintenance Tips for AC Repair

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Rinse the outdoor condenser coil each spring and keep vegetation two feet back
  • Change the filter monthly during cooling season; starved airflow is how coils freeze
  • Start each cooling season by flushing the condensate line so algae cannot block it
  • A new buzz or click at startup often means a weak capacitor, so call before it fails
  • Keep supply and return vents open; closing rooms raises static pressure on the blower
  • Book a professional tune-up each spring before the first heat wave finds the weak parts
  • Shade is fine, but do not box in the outdoor unit with fences or covers
